Does Bitcoin protect your money from inflation?

Educational research. Not financial advice.

Published October 27, 2026 · RealWorth project · Dated research snapshot

RealWorth's finding: Bitcoin earned positive average returns after inflation in both groups in this sample, but its average was lower in high-inflation months and individual outcomes varied widely. This does not establish dependable protection when inflation rises.

What would “protection” mean for your money?

If your savings rise 3% while the things you buy cost 5% more, your account has grown but your buying power has shrunk. An inflation hedge should help when inflation hurts your spending power. A large return over many years is a different claim from reliable protection during a particular inflation shock.

RealWorth calculates purchasing-power returns using the exact ratio: (1 + nominal return) / (1 + inflation) − 1. The figures below are monthly returns after US inflation, not annual returns.

Compare the same observed months

The shared return sample runs from October 2014 through September 2026. “High” means CPI year-over-year inflation was above the sample median of 2.43%; “Low” includes months at or below it. The threshold uses the completed historical sample. This is a retrospective description, not a rule tested using only information available to an investor at the time.

Bitcoin's high-inflation mean was 2.52% per month, but the median was just 0.71%. In everyday language, a few large gains pulled the average above a more typical month's result. The 10th–90th percentile range describes the middle 80% of historical observations; it is not a promised range for the next month.

What does this mean for a decision?

“Inflation is high, so Bitcoin must rise” is not supported by this comparison. Gold's narrower observed spread also does not make it loss-free. If the money is needed for rent, tuition or an emergency, a positive historical average does not remove the risk of a loss before the bill arrives.

For genuinely spare money, these figures can inform how much uncertainty you are willing to study. They do not identify a current entry price. Use What fits my money? to separate the purpose of your money from a short-term market story.

What this comparison cannot tell us

High inflation and rising inflation are different questions. The inflation evidence tool lets you examine level, quartile and rising-versus-falling groups separately. Group differences do not isolate causation: interest rates, equity risk appetite and other events also changed.

Returns are in US dollars, before personal taxes and trading costs. The sample is heavily influenced by Bitcoin's expansion. Official October 2025 CPI is missing; it is not filled in. Real monthly returns requiring that observation are excluded, leaving 70 high-inflation and 72 low-inflation months. Missing data and a changing sample can change the result.
