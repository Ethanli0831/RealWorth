RealWorth explained

What happened to the same $1,000 in five assets?

Educational research. Not financial advice.

Published October 27, 2026 · RealWorth project · Dated research snapshot

RealWorth's finding: Over this particular common history, Bitcoin produced the largest final purchasing-power value and experienced a 76.03% real drawdown. Long-term Treasury bonds lost purchasing power. The largest historical gain does not automatically make an asset suitable for your savings.

Use one starting line

Comparing Bitcoin from one year with gold from another can confuse asset choice with date choice. Here every asset starts at the September 2014 month-end and finishes at the September 2026 month-end. Each starts with $1,000, distributions in the adjusted histories are reinvested, and all values are in US dollars.

“Final real value” means the amount expressed in the starting month's purchasing power. A final $2,000 means twice the buying power of the original $1,000, rather than a forecast of what a future bank balance will show. These are historical examples before personal taxes and trading costs.

On a phone, swipe the table sideways to see every column.

A final profit can hide a painful journey

A drawdown is the fall from an earlier peak. Bitcoin's 76.03% figure means that a $1,000 purchasing-power value at an observed peak would have fallen to roughly $240 at a later observed trough. That is not necessarily the loss from the original purchase date. Monthly observations also miss losses and recoveries inside each month.

The final-value column alone cannot tell you whether you could have tolerated the path, needed the money during a fall, or bought just before a peak. In the five-asset calculator , change the buy month and compare the same holding period. Check both the final result and the time spent below the starting purchasing power.

What if you held for three years instead?

The final two columns repeat a 36-month hold from each eligible month in the common sample. There are 108 usable windows per asset and one omitted window with a missing CPI endpoint. Each return compares the purchasing power at the end with the start, even if an interior CPI observation is absent.

Bitcoin beat inflation in 99.07% of those windows, yet its worst window lost 10.57% of starting purchasing power. SPY beat inflation in every observed three-year window in this sample. Neither result is a future success probability: adjacent windows overlap for 35 months and share much of the same market history.

What should a beginner take away?

Money needed soon and money you can leave untouched for years face different risks. A high long-run return cannot pay a near-term bill if you must sell after a crash. BIL's relatively stable nominal path did not preserve purchasing power over this entire period; TLT's long maturity exposed it to substantial interest-rate risk. “Treasury” does not mean these two funds behave the same way.

Use the table to ask better questions about your horizon and loss capacity, not to choose whichever row has the biggest number. The rolling holding-period tool shows 1-, 3- and 5-year distributions; the money-purpose screen connects those risks to your stated needs.
