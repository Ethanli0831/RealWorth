RealWorth explained

Why does RealWorth show prices without saying “buy now”?

Educational research. Not financial advice.

Published October 27, 2026 · RealWorth project · Dated research snapshot

RealWorth's finding: In this dated diagnostic, four of five point estimates had higher average error than assuming the price would stay unchanged. One smaller error does not establish a repeatable trading advantage. The scenarios help describe uncertainty; they do not prove a profitable entry.

Start with the simplest possible competitor

Suppose someone predicts tomorrow's Bitcoin price. Before trusting the method, compare it with a very simple guess: tomorrow's price equals today's reference price. This “no-change baseline” costs nothing to calculate. A complicated model should show what it adds beyond that baseline.

RealWorth uses the median and quantiles of 252 earlier, matching-horizon log returns. It does not ask an AI model to invent a price target. The historical diagnostic repeats the calculation at 90 past origins using only completed outcomes known at each origin.

Lower error is better. Bitcoin's model error was 1.267%, compared with 1.260% for no change. On a hypothetical $1,000 reference value, those average absolute percentage errors correspond to about $12.67 and $12.60. They describe the size of past errors, not an expected gain, a worst loss or the cost of a trading strategy.

What does “coverage” actually mean?

The outer range uses the historical 10th and 90th percentiles, with a nominal target of 80%. Coverage asks how often an observed outcome fell inside that range. It does not ask whether the prediction got the direction right. A very wide range can cover many outcomes and still be unhelpful for choosing a trade.

In this snapshot, TLT's coverage was 67.78%, below that nominal target. Bitcoin's 86.67% was above it. Those measured proportions come from a small, dependent historical test; neither number is a calibrated probability for tomorrow.

Why the test is not the same as a trading record

The 90 test origins and their training samples overlap, and two-session outcomes overlap as well. Bitcoin's historical diagnostic uses daily closes as a proxy for its published intraday 24-hour targets. ETF scenarios run from a completed close to the relevant scheduled session close, sometimes spanning two sessions. These horizons must not be silently treated as identical.

The separate published forecast ledger freezes a scenario before its target and checks it afterward. Look at results per asset and at the number of distinct target dates. More recorded issues on one date do not create more independent market days. Price-only scenarios also omit distributions, personal taxes, trading spreads and fees.

What can you do with a scenario?

Use it to ask how a plausible adverse move might affect money you need soon. Read the quote time, frozen anchor time, target time and stale-data notices. Current news and AI explanations are additional context; they do not turn an unproven price model into a trading edge.

If the goal is quick, dependable profit, this evidence does not provide a reliable way to achieve it. Longer-term suitability is a separate question: an asset might deserve further research for spare money while still offering no justified “buy today” signal. What fits my money? keeps those questions separate.
