Understand the numbers

Educational research. Not financial advice.

How we measure
what money keeps.

RealWorth compares historical purchasing power, the risk of holding through losses, and returns during different inflation regimes. Every number comes from the same generated dataset. No metric is a recommendation to buy an asset.

Sources and the sample

Daily adjusted closes come from Yahoo Finance through yfinance and are sampled at each month’s last available close. Adjustments include ETF distributions. The study begins in January 2014; Yahoo’s Bitcoin history begins in September 2014. CPI comes from FRED’s seasonally adjusted US consumer price index, CPIAUCSL. Only completed months through the latest released CPI observation are used.

Asset Yahoo ticker What it represents
Bitcoin BTC-USD Bitcoin in US dollars
Gold GLD Gold ETF, not physical gold ownership
S&P 500 SPY US stock-market ETF
T-Bills BIL Short-term US Treasury bill ETF
LT Bonds TLT Long-term US Treasury bond ETF

BIL is the risk-free proxy in the Sharpe calculation because it holds very short Treasury bills. It is still an ETF: fees, price changes, and inflation affect its returns. TLT holds long-duration coupon-paying Treasury bonds, which can fall sharply when interest rates rise.

1. Real-return calculator

A buy month means buying at that month’s end. One year means 12 months later. “Until latest data” ends at the dataset’s last month, not today’s market price. If a selected period runs beyond the available data, we ask you to shorten it.

nominal return[t] = price[t] / price[t−1] − 1
inflation[t] = CPI[t] / CPI[t−1] − 1
real return[t] = (1 + nominal return[t]) / (1 + inflation[t]) − 1

nominal growth[t] = price[t] / price[baseline]
real growth[t] = nominal growth[t] × CPI[baseline] / CPI[t]

holding return = growth[end] / growth[buy] − 1
end value = amount invested × growth[end] / growth[buy]

The website uses the exact inflation adjustment, approved for this independent project, rather than the paper’s approximation nominal return − inflation. Real investment value is expressed in buy-month dollars: it tells you how much purchasing power your investment kept relative to when you bought.

Worst holding-period drawdown is the largest observed drop from any earlier real-value peak within that holding period. The lowest dollar value during a hold is a separate calculation; it is not necessarily the trough of the largest percentage drawdown.

2. Store-of-Value Scorecard

Each asset uses its own available history. This means Bitcoin and the ETFs have different start dates. Annual real return is geometric growth, not an arithmetic average of unusually strong months.

CAGR = final real growth^(12 / elapsed calendar months) − 1
volatility = sample standard deviation(monthly real returns) × √12

excess[t] = asset real return[t] − BIL real return[t]
Sharpe = √12 × mean(excess) / sample standard deviation(excess)

drawdown[t] = real growth[t] / prior running peak[t] − 1
max drawdown = minimum(drawdown)
Calmar = real CAGR / absolute value(max drawdown)
current drawdown = latest real growth / all-time observed peak − 1

Sample standard deviations use ddof=1. BIL’s own excess returns are zero, so its Sharpe is undefined, not zero. A Calmar ratio is also undefined when there is no drawdown; the site displays “Not defined”.

Verdicts are descriptive rules. A negative real CAGR is “Loses to inflation”. Otherwise, a drawdown worse than −40% is “Positive return, deep drawdowns”; −15% to −40% is “Grows, with drawdown risk”; better than −15% is “Preserved purchasing power”. Colour labels supplement the numbers: mild, significant, and severe. These rules do not certify future safety.

3. Inflation Regime Tracker

CPI YoY[t] = CPI[t] / CPI[t−12] − 1
High: CPI YoY > sample median
Low: CPI YoY ≤ sample median
Bottom 25%: CPI YoY ≤ Q25
Top 25%: CPI YoY ≥ Q75
Middle 50%: between Q25 and Q75

The median and quartiles use months with nominal returns for all five assets and known CPI YoY, normally October 2014 onward. Quartiles use linear interpolation. Thresholds are recalculated as new months arrive. Group averages are arithmetic means of nominal monthly returns, before inflation. They are not real returns or annualised numbers. A relationship between returns and inflation is not evidence that inflation caused those returns.

Missing observations stay missing

FRED has no CPI observation for October 2025. We do not invent one. That month’s real growth and CPI YoY are null in the JSON and shown as a gap in charts. If a buy or end month has missing CPI, the calculator reports nominal value but marks real value “No data”.

When CPI is known again, real growth resumes using its ratio to the baseline CPI. Known endpoints allow an exact multi-month return even with an unknown intermediate month. CAGR still counts every elapsed calendar month. Monthly volatility and Sharpe exclude both the missing CPI month and its following month: the two-month move is never treated as one monthly return.

Drawdowns use known observations. An unknown month might hide a higher peak or lower trough, so the reported decline may understate the true worst fall. Missing YoY observations are excluded from regime groups; if the prior-year CPI is missing, even a known current CPI cannot establish a current YoY regime.

4. Economic weather and debt-cycle signals

The supplementary US economic-weather panel uses real GDP YoY growth versus the previous quarter and CPI YoY versus six months earlier, with a ±0.10 percentage-point deadband. These asynchronous observations describe direction; they do not measure surprises against consensus or forecast returns. Flat or unknown inputs produce a mixed/incomplete reading.

Short-cycle warning flags are our heuristics: a high-yield spread of at least 5 percentage points, or a three-month unemployment average at least 0.5 points above its trailing 12-month minimum. A policy-rate change of at least ±0.25 points over three months distinguishes tightening/easing. Stale daily/monthly/quarterly periods are excluded after 10/75/200 days respectively. The GDP, inflation, policy, unemployment and spread evidence must be usable for a short-cycle label.

This is inspired by Dalio’s distinction between credit expansion, repayment constraints and deleveraging. It is not a Bridgewater model or a diagnosis of a full long-term debt-cycle phase. Private credit, public debt and household debt service are different measures; currency denomination, maturity, external balances and policy capacity require further investigation. Our research directory includes public and subscription reading sources; it does not scrape paywalled reports.

5. News impact chains

A separate approximately 15-minute GitHub workflow reads selected public publisher and institutional RSS feeds. It retains up to 60 dated, keyword-matched headlines from the last 14 days, with publisher, link, date and source-health status. A separate hourly AI task reads selected public article bodies, respecting publisher robots rules and paywalls. Full bodies are not published. Missing feeds retain explicitly cached headlines with their original dates; an entirely failed refresh preserves the previous file. Source failures and stale snapshots remain visible.

Headline keywords select prewritten conditional mechanism chains. These are neither verified causal effects nor independent factual verification of article claims. They show intermediate channels, competing effects, relevant time horizons and evidence that could weaken a hypothesis. They do not estimate impact sizes, target prices, probabilities or investment recommendations.

The artificial-gold example points to a historical CERN primary source as a possible match for the user-described French story. Producing nuclei does not by itself demonstrate commercially competitive gold supply. A separate French announcement is unconfirmed; Bitcoin substitution needs separate demand and flow evidence. The case is labelled historical and excluded from the recent-headline feed unless a genuinely recent publisher item matches.

6. AI-assisted article reading and next-period price scenarios

Actual model inference is separate from headline-rule templates. Selected readable public articles are extracted, hashed and presented as untrusted input to a model. GitHub Models is attempted with the scoped workflow token; public Qwen CPU inference is the no-user-key fallback. Each accepted output must cite actual short source fragments, include conditional channels and disclose uncertainty. The model name, provider, revision and read/analysis timestamps identify its provenance. Model context contains at most the first 4,500 extracted characters plus short source fragments; extracted and supplied lengths are shown separately. Truncation can omit important context. Structural validation does not certify factual correctness, and model errors remain possible. No tools execute article instructions, no paywall is bypassed and no complete publisher bodies are published.

Conditional financial and asset explanations must state an explicit “If” condition. For bodies without Bitcoin mentions, its direction is restricted to Mixed/Unclear; competing risk/liquidity and possible hedge demand remain hypotheses. Each hourly run attempts at most two new interpretations. Reported figures must match a source headline or quoted fragment; numerical asset targets are rejected. These scope and syntax checks do not establish economic causality.

Numerical scenarios use 252 completed price log returns matched to the anchor-to-target span (one or two trading sessions). Lower, central and upper bands correspond to Q10–Q30, Q40–Q60 and Q70–Q90, with Q50 as the central point. They are historical quantile scenarios, not guaranteed probabilities or AI-invented price targets. Related AI news context does not mechanically change the bands. Gold is GLD per ETF share, not spot gold per ounce.

Bitcoin targets roughly 24 hours after a frozen observed quote, rounded up to a five-minute boundary. ETFs use the last completed close and next NYSE-calendar session close on or after tomorrow, including holidays and early closes. During a trading session this spans two closes from the last completed anchor, so two-session historical returns are used. Already issued forecasts remain frozen. Quote snapshots update independently on a roughly 15-minute schedule; publisher, exchange and GitHub delays apply.

The 90-observation walk-forward diagnostic uses 252 matching-horizon returns whose endpoints were available at each historical origin, and compares median-price error with a no-change random walk. This is not future performance; for Bitcoin it is a daily-close proxy rather than calibration of the exact intraday 24-hour horizon. Overlapping horizons and multiple issues for the same asset/target are correlated; aggregate results are descriptive.

Prospective verification waits for the target: an ETF session close or the first completed Bitcoin five-minute bar no more than five minutes after target. A missing target is marked unverified, never filled with another day's result. Archived outcomes contain realized returns and price errors; original forecast records remain auditable in Git history. The first release has pending outcomes, so no measured prospective accuracy exists yet.

What this research cannot tell you

  • The sample starts in 2014 and includes major Bitcoin bull markets. Later results can differ substantially.
  • Only US CPI is used. Results do not describe purchasing power in every country or currency.
  • Month-end observations miss intra-month crashes. CPI gaps add further uncertainty to drawdowns.
  • Taxes, transaction costs, custody costs and personal inflation are not modelled. ETF management costs are reflected in adjusted prices.
  • Yahoo and FRED may revise historical observations. Regime thresholds and computed results can change.
  • Past performance and regime averages do not predict future returns.

Reproducibility and updates

scripts/build_data.py downloads data, validates it and atomically writes docs/data/data.json. Downloads get three attempts; a failure preserves the last working file. The site publishes derived growth indices and metrics, not raw Yahoo price series. The source repository explains local use.

GitHub Actions refreshes on the 18th of each month at 06:00 UTC, also supports manual runs, and checks changes pushed to main. Only a changed JSON file is committed by the monthly job. It explicitly requests a GitHub Pages rebuild so the refreshed data reaches the website.

Based on the research paper: “Digital Gold or High-Beta Gamble? Evaluating Bitcoin as a Store of Value and Inflation Hedge”, Ethan (GitHub: Ethanli0831), 2026. Explain it in your own words

A five-question self-check

This is a learning exercise, not an automatically graded test. Write your answer, then compare it with the explanation. One question at a time.

Comparable histories and rolling holding periods

The scorecard defaults to the first and last month where all five assets have nominal and real index levels. Each real path is rebased at that common starting month. Annual growth uses elapsed calendar months; volatility uses the sample standard deviation of genuine consecutive real monthly returns, multiplied by √12. Sharpe uses real returns in excess of contemporaneous BIL, divided by the sample standard deviation of that excess. Switching to full history deliberately restores unequal asset start dates.

For holding length h, every eligible month t contributes a cumulative real return G(real,t+h) / G(real,t) − 1. The historical beat-inflation share counts returns strictly above zero. We report the number of windows, omitted missing endpoints, worst return, median and P10/P90 interpolated empirical quantiles. The windows overlap and are not independent; the fraction is not a forecast probability. Interior CPI gaps do not prevent a known-endpoint return but can hide path risk.

Five-asset comparison and purchasing-power recovery

All paths use the same amount, buy month and holding end, expressed in buy-month dollars. Recovery means the first observed month back at or above purchase purchasing power after a prior observed loss, measured in months from purchase. It is distinct from never falling below purchase, not recovering within the chosen period, or missing starting CPI. A later relapse is possible. No missing-month crossing is invented. Invalid holding periods are disabled before submission.

Inflation distributions: level and direction are distinct

High/Low and quartiles use the original unrounded CPI YoY observations and shared nominal-return months. The real-return view additionally requires actual consecutive CPI endpoints; a missing CPI month and its successor have no one-month real return. Each group shows asset-level sample size, mean, median, P10/P90 and minimum/maximum. The default is real return; nominal returns remain available. The direction test compares this month's CPI YoY with the preceding calendar month's YoY: Rising is strictly greater; Falling / flat includes ties. A missing prior observation is excluded. It is a separate descriptive test, not a forecast or proof of an inflation hedge.

News relevance and event grouping

Headline routing applies conservative context checks before assigning a mechanism. Standalone words such as jobs and war are insufficient. Career advice, individual fraud and similar stories can display No clear macro impact, without a template or AI interpretation. Company layoffs do not by themselves establish economy-wide weakness. Known employer/action matches and near-identical titles within three days can group reports, retaining individual source links and dates. This conservative grouping can miss duplicates or combine successive closely related updates; it is not verified event identification. Selected readable relevant articles may proceed to actual AI, whose hypotheses still require source review.

Price evidence, baseline and repeated issues

Historical model median error is shown beside unchanged-price error before the scenario cards. Smaller mean absolute percentage error is better. Equal or worse errors provide no demonstrated advantage over that baseline. An outer interval covering an outcome does not mean the model predicted its direction correctly. Prospective scores are separated by asset and use the earliest issue for each target date in America/Toronto; total verified issues and distinct target dates are shown separately. This prevents repeated same-date issues receiving extra weight, but dates remain statistically dependent and horizons can differ. Archived actual returns reconstruct baseline error as |r / (1+r)| × 100, where r is the realised fractional return. Frozen original issues and all later outcomes stay in the ledger.

Market-structure sources and coverage

The Market Monitor reads selected public fund and market datasets separately from Yahoo monthly prices: IBIT official bitcoin holdings; GLD official tonnes; Deribit BTC perpetual funding, USD open interest and DVOL; Coin Metrics community active addresses, transaction counts, BTC fees and estimated hash rate; FRED M2SL and DTWEXBGS; and CFTC futures-only gold managed-money net positions. Each reading reports its own observation date, source, fetch status and limitations. Failed sources retain dated old observations or explicit No data. No secret keys are required.

Funding and open-interest history is collected prospectively one observation per date, not fabricated retroactively. A trailing-year rank requires at least 250 daily observations (48 weekly CFTC or 12 monthly M2 observations), spanning at least 330 days; shorter histories show no percentile. It is an empirical rank, not a calibrated tail probability. Fund holdings and AUM are not dollar net flows. The GLD comparison rebases official price and holdings to a common starting date; it does not infer causation. World Gold Council physical-demand breakdowns, comparable option skew and CAD purchasing power are listed as research sources or not connected; they are not implied by the connected indicators. US CPI remains the inflation basis of the return tools.

Money-purpose guidance: needs before returns

The optional What fits my money? questionnaire asks the purpose, earliest spending horizon, nominal loss capacity, emergency/debt readiness, spending currency and goal. Amount is used only to illustrate the scale of a loss, not to infer wealth, expertise or an allocation. Answers remain in page memory, are not persisted, and are not sent to an AI service. The recommendation is generated by disclosed deterministic rules, not a new language-model prediction.

Essential or borrowed money, a zero-loss requirement, missing emergency reserves, expensive debt and unknown readiness take priority over a willingness to speculate. Quick-profit requests do not receive a buy signal: prediction errors, interval coverage and a small improvement over unchanged prices do not establish a profitable strategy after trading costs. Spending in CAD or another non-USD currency is marked unassessed because these return tools use USD and US CPI. No exchange conversion, local product rate, tax outcome, insured-deposit guarantee or future market return is invented.

For the remaining comparisons, policy minimum horizons are 3 years for Bitcoin/gold and 5 years for SPY/TLT. The nominal loss stress floors are Bitcoin 100%, gold 35%, SPY 40%, BIL 5% and TLT 40%, never less than the observed common-period nominal maximum drawdown. These are conservative screening assumptions, not calibrated future loss limits or data-derived estimates. Bitcoin is limited to explicitly disposable, potentially fully lost experimental money. Stable-income requests do not reclassify Bitcoin, gold, equities or long-bond funds as dependable fixed payouts. BIL is not an insured deposit and has variable distributions.

The engine requires at least 12 matching historical holding windows for a comparison. Overlapping windows are dependent; their inflation-beating fraction is descriptive, not a probability of success. For deadlines shorter than one year, one-year history is labelled background only and is not represented as days/weeks evidence. All history uses identical observed endpoints across the five assets. Nominal and real drawdowns are shown separately, and historical stress amounts are explicitly examples rather than expected losses. A complete sample ending before older crises can understate potential future risk.

Current dated macro growth/inflation and credit/employment flags can explain caution, but cannot time an entry. Missing or stale macro/quote data never supplies a positive signal. Core research older than 75 days since refresh or 110 days from its final month, malformed data or missing common history produces no asset clearance. A card may identify a smaller historical error while still saying no profitable trading edge has been demonstrated. Worth comparing is not a recommendation to buy today.

Every asset explanation provides the actual shared sample dates, historical nominal/real drawdowns, matching window count, worst final real return and inflation-beating share, plus a readable Research link and derived JSON evidence. Published outcome counts are distinct from historical backtests. A dollar range illustration scales the already frozen starting quote and Q10/Q90 levels, not the newest quote; it is not a return forecast for entering now. No entry price, leverage instruction or portfolio percentage is prescribed. This five-asset, self-reported screen is not a complete professional suitability assessment.

Purpose fit, exposure size and entry timing are separate

A missing next-period trading edge is not a blanket conclusion that a compatible long-term investment should be avoided. Eligible uses can be worth researching; insufficient loss capacity for an all-in position can instead lead to a reduced-exposure illustration. Essential money, borrowing, zero-loss needs, missing financial basics, capital/income incompatibility, horizon and data requirements are checked first. Quick-profit requests receive an observation-only strategy verdict rather than declaring every asset intrinsically unsuitable.

For an eligible USD comparison, let A be the total money in this plan, L the stated acceptable total loss percentage, and S the screening stress percentage. The illustrative exposure fraction is min(1, L/S). Risk amount = A × fraction; the remainder is assumed to be unchanged cash; stress loss = risk amount × S/100. For A = $1,000, L = 15% and S = 40%, the independent scenario is $375 exposed and $625 unchanged, with a $150 assumed loss. It is not a recommended allocation or a guaranteed loss cap: greater losses, fees, inflation, currency moves and different cash/portfolio behaviour can exceed the budget.

Each asset card is an alternative single-asset-plus-cash scenario. Do not add the amounts from different cards together. A real multi-asset portfolio requires a combined assessment, including correlations and existing holdings. Non-USD users can review conditional USD evidence, but local purchasing power remains unassessed and no local-currency amount budget is produced. The research shortlist is no longer restricted to whether SPY alone qualifies. No calibration or return guarantee is claimed for these policy choices.
