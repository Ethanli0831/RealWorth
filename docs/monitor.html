The range describes uncertainty. Q10–Q90 coverage is the share of outcomes inside the outer interval, not the accuracy of predicting up or down.

How prices, market closures and forecasts are handled

Bitcoin trades continuously: each day’s first forecast uses a reported quote and targets roughly 24 hours later, rounded up to the next five-minute boundary. ETF scenarios use the last completed daily close and the first NYSE-calendar close on or after tomorrow, including holidays and early closes. If tomorrow has no ETF session, the target explicitly moves to the next trading close; no Sunday price is fabricated.

Forecasts are issued once per Bitcoin Eastern-calendar day or ETF target and completed anchor combination, and remain unchanged. A new completed close can create a separate issue for the same target. A forecast issued earlier is not an up-to-the-minute conditional model. The latest quote is shown separately with its original source time. Training uses 252 completed one- or two-session price-only returns matched to the actual anchor-to-target span, excluding cash distributions. No forecasting advantage over a random walk is established.

Public Yahoo histories are processed transiently; complete raw price arrays are not published. Requested current quote points and derived scenario prices are published. After verification, the archive retains derived realized returns and errors instead of raw closing-price histories.

News source health & coverage

    Only dated, relevant headlines in the last 14 days are retained, capped at 60. Matching uses headline keywords and can miss context, negation or important stories. A fetched feed can have zero relevant items. Articles are not ranked by expected investment return.
