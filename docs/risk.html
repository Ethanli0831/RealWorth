Three questions before acting

  1. What is actually known? A headline is a lead. A campaign promise, proposed bill, enacted rule and effective restriction are different things.
  2. How could it reach my holdings? Look at revenue, costs, valuation, rates, currencies, leverage and supplier/customer links. An election result alone is not a sell signal.
  3. Can this money tolerate the uncertainty? A near-term bill or concentrated position needs a different review from diversified money you can leave invested for years.

What deserves a closer look?

Exposure, not geographic immunity

How could a US shock reach Asia or Europe?

Economic cycles can differ while export orders, funding, currencies and portfolio flows remain connected. These are general channels to investigate, not measured causes of a particular decline or predicted index-loss ranges.

Three different AI scenarios

US-specific policy constraint

New projects might be delayed, redesigned or built elsewhere. Customer location, power, data rules, export controls and market access limit substitution. Overseas suppliers can still lose orders.

Global AI returns disappoint

Revenue and cash flow might fail to justify spending or valuations. That could affect AI suppliers across regions. Useful technology does not automatically make every related stock good value.

Broader funding or demand shock

Credit stress or recession could affect sectors beyond AI. Assets may be sold for liquidity. A loss in market value is repricing, not a cash pot that must move into Chinese or European shares.

These are alternative hypotheses. No probabilities, automatic destination for capital, or numerical market-loss range is assigned.
